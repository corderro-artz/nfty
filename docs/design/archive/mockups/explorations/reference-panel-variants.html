nfty · exploration · reference-layer panel

Surface: the Ingredient Editor's right rail in ingredient-editor.html, beneath Colorize — a new panel that composites 0..N other layers at their real depths onto the paint canvas so art can be lined up before it is drawn. Context throughout is VaporPets › Aurora › Eyes, pinned at depth 3 of 4: Body and Ears composite under it, Accessory over it. Each variant is drawn three times — nothing on (the default), three siblings on, and a loose Kitchen .igt placed into the stack.

These are explorations, not locked. Nothing here belongs to the 1:1 reference set in docs/design/mockups/. One variant is promoted; the other two are deleted.

The stakes

What the panel drives — the paint canvas itself

Not the corner preview blip: the spec's performance note (RebuildSurfaces(), a pre-composited below-stack and above-stack, two DrawImage calls per stroke) puts the references on the surface being painted. Grey is the value-map you are drawing; colour is a reference.

Variant A

Stack ladder + Kitchen scratch tray

The idea. One ladder that is the paint stack, topmost layer first, with the edited layer pinned in the middle of it — so "above" and "below" need no label, they are just up and down. Loose Kitchen files are held in a dashed scratch tray beneath the ladder and lift into it as dashed ghost rows when switched on. Two containers, two materials: solid rows are the real composition, dashed rows are scratch.
The trade-off. The truest mental model of the composite — and the only variant where a Kitchen layer's relationship to the art is visible without reading anything. But it lists depth 4 → 1 downward, the inverse of the Layers table in explorer.html, so the two surfaces in the same app would disagree about which way a stack is drawn.

Variant B

Two labelled sections, depth gutter

The idea. Two headed sections — In this recipe and From the Kitchen, the second carrying the titlebar's own .kroot Kitchen chip — each a list of filmstrip cards with a leading checkbox. The gutter to its left is where the groups separate a second time: a sibling shows a real depth numeral, a Kitchen file shows a placement stepper and no numeral at all, because it has no canonical depth to show.
The trade-off. Cheapest to build, most conventional, and it keeps explorer.html's ascending 1→N reading. But the above/below consequence is not spatial — rows 1 and 2 sit above row 3 on screen while compositing beneath it — so each active row has to carry an explicit over/under tag to undo the contradiction.

Variant C

Above / Below zones, provenance per row

The idea. The pin becomes a divider and the panel's primary axis becomes the consequence: composites over above it, composites under below it. Source is demoted to a per-row read carried three redundant ways — a dashed row border, the Kitchen chip, and an empty depth gutter. Only Kitchen rows get the ▴▾ stepper, because only they can move; a sibling's depth belongs to the Recipe, not to this preview.
The trade-off. The consequence is unmissable and there is nowhere for a scratch layer to hide. But "sibling or scratch?" becomes a per-row read rather than a spatial one, and real depth ordering within a zone is much weaker than in A. And look at rail 1, drawn rather than argued: because every Kitchen file defaults to "on top" and C gives scratch nowhere to wait, the default panel opens with four dimmed files stacked above the first real layer. C needs an answer for where an unplaced Kitchen file lives — A's tray is one; a collapsed "add from Kitchen" row is another.

look at panel 3 → In every variant the third rail places the Kitchen file shades.igt at over Eyes — and the top preview shows the result: the sunglasses completely cover the eyes you are painting. That is the spec followed literally ("layers above composite over it"), and it is drawn here rather than argued about, because it is the one thing the feature cannot ship without an answer to.
